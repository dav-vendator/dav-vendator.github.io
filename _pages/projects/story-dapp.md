    <h2>Project Overview</h2>
    <p>
      StoryDapp is an older full-stack blockchain project built around the idea
      of decentralized story publishing. The project explores how written content
      can be stored, published, and interacted with through smart contracts and a
      decentralized application interface.
    </p>

    <p>
      While this project is not part of my current research direction, it
      represents an important stage in my technical journey: experimenting with
      blockchain systems, smart contract workflows, frontend integration, and
      distributed application design.
    </p>

    <div class="tech-spec-callout">
      <h3>// Toolchain</h3>
      <ul>

        <li><strong>Focus:</strong> Smart contracts, publishing workflows, and digital ownership</li>

      </ul>
    </div>

    <h2>Core Components</h2>

    <h3>1. Decentralized Publishing</h3>
    <p>
      The project experiments with publishing stories through a decentralized
      application model rather than a conventional centralized platform.
    </p>

    <h3>2. Smart Contract Interaction</h3>
    <p>
      StoryDapp explores how application logic can be encoded and triggered
      through blockchain-based smart contracts.
    </p>

    <h3>3. Web3 Frontend Integration</h3>
    <p>
      The application connects user-facing interface logic with blockchain
      interactions, giving practical exposure to the Web3 development stack.
    </p>

    <h3>4. Ownership and Digital Content</h3>
    <p>
      The project investigates the idea of user-owned digital writing and how
      content platforms can be designed around decentralized ownership models.
    </p>

    <h2>Why I Built This</h2>
    <p>
      StoryDapp was built as an exploration of blockchain beyond currency. I was
      interested in how decentralized systems could be used for publishing,
      authorship, and ownership of creative work.
    </p>

    <p>
      Even though my current focus has shifted toward quantum machine learning,
      numerical computing, and systems programming, this project remains useful
      as evidence of my broader experimentation with emerging computing
      paradigms.
    </p>
